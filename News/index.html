iPhone + iPad: DarkSword iOS exploit kit now steals keychain and wallet data

A new P7 variant of the DarkSword iOS exploit chain has been used in the wild since November 2025 and targets iOS 18.4 through 18.7. It now steals keychain and crypto wallet data and adds two-way remote commands. It spreads through a re-registered analytics domain that injects JavaScript into sites still loading the expired tag, and the article says DarkSword does not cover iOS 26.

All users: Fake Claude installers in search ads use ClickFix trick

Attackers are buying Google Ads and abusing Bing redirects to send people searching for Claude to fake installer pages. Those pages use ClickFix social engineering to get victims to run malicious commands themselves. The campaign is active in the wild.

All users: Phone-call extortion gang stole data from 27 law firms

Silent Ransom Group allegedly extorted about $207 million from 27 law firms. It used phone calls and social engineering to get access and steal data, and did not encrypt any files. The case shows callback and vishing-based data extortion hitting professional-services firms.

Microsoft: October 2026 security updates

MSRC republished 39 existing Windows CVE entries. They include remote code execution flaws in NTFS, Netlogon (CVE-2026-72982), DNS (CVE-2026-72987), DHCP Server (CVE-2026-72979), Media Foundation and others, plus about 26 elevation-of-privilege flaws. These are revisions of existing entries with no reports of exploitation, and the fixes are in previously released updates.

Windows + Server: Outdated devices lose Windows Update after 2027 certificate rotation

The certificates Windows uses to verify Windows Update servers expire on May 17 and June 19, 2027, and devices that are not current will stop getting patches. Windows 11 24H2 and Server 2025 need the September 2025 update or later, and Server 2019, Server 2016 and Windows 10 Enterprise 2019 LTSC need the July 2026 update or later. Windows 11 25H2 and later, and devices updated through WSUS, are not affected (MC1491763).

Microsoft Teams: QR codes in external senders' images hidden by default

An updated M365 Roadmap entry says QR codes inside images sent by external senders in Teams will be obscured by default to protect against QR phishing. General availability is targeted for October 2026, and other sources give a mid-October rollout.
